A survival game
with a sense of theater.

I built Inkshot’s combat, progression and multiplayer systems as a solo project. Each wave asks the player to balance immediate survival with the weapon they want to build next.

The local development version combines a twelve-wave campaign with personal upgrades, resource gathering and buildable cover. Its illustrated characters and theater venues give those systems a world of their own.

Behind the curtain

The engineering work connects moment-to-moment combat, each player’s progression and a shared multiplayer world.

One authority for the shared world.

The host runs combat, wave progression and venue transitions. Guests send movement and action requests over Unity Relay’s secure WebSocket transport; the host validates actions before updating the shared state.

  1. GuestRequests an action
  2. HostValidates the request
  3. PlayersReceive world updates

Rooms support co-op or versus and are designed for up to four players. The room closes when its host leaves.

Personal progression, shared consequences.

Players keep separate weapon classes, inventories, coins and purchases while sharing combat and wave outcomes. Gathering, crafting and cover placement connect those personal decisions to the physical arena.

The host checks guest gathering and building requests. This keeps inventory spending and world changes under the same authority as the rest of the match.

Development & validation

Saved September 2026 reports cover gameplay rules, inventory isolation, host validation and communication between separate host and guest processes. These development checks are separate from browser-release and load validation.
